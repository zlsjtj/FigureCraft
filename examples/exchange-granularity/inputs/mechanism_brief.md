# Competitive ion exchange inside a hydrogel bead

Create a publication-quality mechanism figure for the following independent
teaching example. It is not related to the RPC paper. All populations are fixed
illustrative counts, not measurements. Provide an editable figure and an export.
The figure will be placed at 160 mm width, with every necessary label at least
8 pt at that width. A restrained 2.5D treatment is allowed. Composition, graphical
primitives, and visual style are left open.

A hydrated spherical polymer bead contains immobilized monovalent negative
binding groups. The bead has three concentric permeable regions: shell, middle,
and core. Their radii, expressed relative to the bead radius, are 0.80 to 1.00,
0.45 to 0.80, and 0.00 to 0.45. The capacities below are exact illustrative counts
of binding groups; they are not inferred from region volume. All regions are
connected through water-filled pores and communicate with one common external
well-mixed bath. The three regions do not have separate external baths.

Initially each negative group is occupied by one Na+ counterion. The bath
contains 40 Ca2+ ions and 80 Cl- ions, with zero Na+. At the depicted later state,
Ca2+ has entered the bead. One bound Ca2+ occupies two negative groups in the same
region; each released Na+ frees one group. Thus Ca2+ and Na+ compete for the same
finite group population rather than binding to independent species-specific
capacities. Every group remains occupied in both depicted states. The simplified
model has no H+, no anion binding, no precipitation, and no mobile ions remaining
inside pore water at either depicted snapshot. Water and polymer mass are not
counted. Chloride remains in the external bath as the spectator counterion.

At the later snapshot, the shell has 8 bound Ca2+ and 8 bound Na+; the middle
has 6 bound Ca2+ and 24 bound Na+; the core has 2 bound Ca2+ and 16 bound Na+.
The bath consequently has 24 Ca2+, 32 Na+, and 80 Cl-. The counts refer to the
single bead and its single bath; no duplication of ions is implied by any view.
The site-sharing stoichiometry is 2 S-Na + Ca2+ -> S2-Ca + 2 Na+, where S denotes
one immobilized negative group. The two S groups in a Ca complex lie in the same
region. A complex must not connect two different regions.

The later state is an illustrative transient. Its spatial distribution is given,
not derived from region geometry, equilibrium selectivity, or a measured rate.
No comparison of exchange speed, diffusion constant, or performance is available.
`region_populations.csv` fixes the regional mapping and `system_balance.csv`
fixes system totals. The visual should communicate the bead, connected regions,
competition for shared groups, direction of net exchange, and these quantitative
relationships accurately. Do not add unprovided scientific conclusions.

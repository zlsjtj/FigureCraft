# Construction and bench log (DEMO)

The fixture was assembled using an existing compression frame, its force and displacement sensors, a rigid rectangular upper contact pad on a single transverse pivot pin, a friction clamp that can hold the pivot angle, and a fixed lower platen. These components were available before this study. The frame controller, force calibration, pad surface finish, sensor sampling, and analysis software are unchanged between modes. No new joint or feedback control algorithm was built.

The upper crosshead carries the pivot, whose axis is perpendicular to the side-view plane. The pad can pitch about this one axis, but it cannot roll about a second axis. Its flat lower face presses the coupon against the fixed lower platen. A lock marker in a schematic can denote the friction clamp; screw count, internal clamp geometry, and dimensions are not provided. The pad, coupon, pivot, and lower platen are the relevant physical objects. A side view can show pitch alignment. Perspective can help distinguish surfaces, but is schematic and not a measured CAD model.

The run notebook uses these mode codes:
- E: start with the upper pad horizontal; tighten the angular clamp before approaching the coupon. Then approach until 10 N, zero the displacement increment, and ramp 0.20 mm. The pad angle remains fixed.
- F: leave the angular clamp open during approach to 10 N, zero the displacement increment, and keep it open during the same 0.20 mm ramp.
- L: leave the angular clamp open during approach to 10 N; hold that preload, tighten the same clamp without lifting the pad, then zero the displacement increment and ramp 0.20 mm with the clamp closed. The record requires a preload-hold acknowledgement before the lock step; missing acknowledgement aborts the run. No records here lack acknowledgement. No lock-force measurement or automated correction is made.

The 10 N preload and 0.20 mm increment came unchanged from the teaching protocol, not a sweep. Each mode is applied to the same fixture and contact pad. The clamp is released only after unloading. L adds an operator action during the preload hold. F and L can settle the pad to a pitch slope; only F stays free to rotate during the ramp. No direct map of contact pressure or quantitative pad-angle trace was recorded.

There are three constructed coupon conditions: flat top; planar top tilted 1 degree along the permitted pitch direction; planar top tilted 1 degree across that direction (roll slope). The last slope cannot be followed by this pivot. Do not draw a ball joint or second rotation axis. No curved coupon, compliant membrane, interchangeable shoe, or fluid is present. Vertical loading does not imply horizontal sliding is intended.

Each row summarizes eight remove-and-reseat cycles of one coupon in that condition. They are repeated mountings, not eight independent material specimens. The same coupon geometry and synthetic inputs were used across modes. Numbers are constructed demonstration values; no new physical test was run. Mode order was not randomized in the fictional notebook. The records do not provide raw force samples or uncertainty intervals.

F_cv_percent is 100 times the sample standard deviation divided by the mean of the force readings at the end of the fixed 0.20 mm ramp across the eight mountings. It measures reseating repeatability of that force, not force accuracy or a modulus. Drift_um is the median magnitude of lateral pad displacement during the ramp. It is not pressure nonuniformity or rotation angle. Seat_s is median elapsed operator time from approach initiation to the start of the ramp, including the hold/lock step when present; unloading and specimen preparation are outside this time. The full test duration was not measured.

For the pitch-tilted coupon an E-mode side view may show contact first near one edge. A settled pad may lie parallel to that top plane. These are illustrative poses implied by the permitted motion, not measured contact maps. A drawn wide gap is an exaggeration for visibility and must be identified as schematic. Cross-slope performance must not be omitted or visually portrayed as corrected. Lower drift does not prove a more uniform pressure field, and repeatability does not prove unbiased material properties.
